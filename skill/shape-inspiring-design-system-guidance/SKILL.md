# Shape Inspiring Design System Guidance

Help people feel the problem, see a credible improvement, and try the better way themselves. The goal is adoption through understanding—not a longer rulebook.

## Workflow

1. Resolve the audience, work object, real decision, evidence boundary, product scope, locale, and one behavior the experience should help change.
2. Start with a recognisable moment of friction. Show its human consequence without fear, shame, invented drama, or a deliberately inaccessible baseline.
3. Build the learning arc in [references/learning-arc-and-cases.md](references/learning-arc-and-cases.md).
4. Keep the work object and underlying facts fixed in a before/after comparison. Change wording, hierarchy, evidence visibility, interaction, and next action—not the truth.
5. For broad concept teaching, include at least three constructive cases with different intents or work objects and one clearly labelled rejected case. Show how to recover from the rejected design.
6. Choose a visual form that reveals the relationship: UI specimen, diagram, chart, map, image, or outline icon. Use text only for the decision or boundary the visual cannot carry.
7. Draft Thai and English independently from the same evidence record. Read [references/humanized-bilingual-copy.md](references/humanized-bilingual-copy.md).
8. Keep the primary route simple. Put detailed rationale, exact rules, source notes, and extended galleries behind clear progressive disclosure.
9. Test comprehension, read-aloud rhythm, action clarity, keyboard and screen-reader access, narrow widths, text zoom, reduced motion, and whether the value remains visible without decorative effects.

## Visual choice

| Relationship to teach | Prefer | Avoid |
|---|---|---|
| Before versus after | paired UI specimens | prose that merely claims improvement |
| Sequence or handoff | short diagram | decorative arrows without a real dependency |
| Quantity or comparison | chart with direct labels | icon counts pretending to be data |
| Place or spatial relation | map or spatial specimen | generic location illustration |
| Real-world context | approved relevant image | stock imagery that changes the evidence |
| Role or action recognition | outline icon with rounded joins when it matches the system | decorative icon saturation |

An icon is a fast entry cue, not a replacement for evidence, state, or an accessible label.

## Boundaries

- Keep shared methodology product-neutral across its intended portfolio. Keep product claims, data, voice, capabilities, and case facts in the owning product layer.
- Compare products, places, or cities only with a compatible schema and release, or name the incompatibility.
- Keep rejected examples readable and usable enough to isolate the real failure.
- Do not turn a preference, implementation experiment, or candidate pattern into a normative rule.
- Do not write slogans that substitute for observable value.
- Do not publish, message, or change external systems unless the user separately authorizes that action.

## Output contract

Return:

1. the audience, work object, decision, and behavior-change goal;
2. the problem-to-action learning arc;
3. the constructive cases and bounded rejected case;
4. the visual/text pairing and detail-on-demand structure;
5. independently authored Thai and English copy when both locales are in scope;
6. preserved evidence and product boundaries;
7. comprehension and accessibility checks still open.

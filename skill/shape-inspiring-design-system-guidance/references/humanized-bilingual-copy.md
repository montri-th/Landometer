# Humanized Thai and English copy

## Draft from evidence, not from translation

Create one fact record first. Draft Thai and English independently from it, then compare factual parity. Do not mirror word order, sentence length, idioms, or punctuation.

## Thai review

- Use words people naturally say at work.
- Name the person, place, object, decision, and next action directly.
- Prefer active verbs over stacked abstract nouns.
- Keep one main thought per sentence; split long official-style clauses.
- Remove translation-shaped phrases, filler politeness, and repeated context.
- Use technical English only when the team genuinely uses it; explain it once when needed.
- Read aloud. Rewrite any line that needs a second pass to understand.
- Keep Thai line breaks at phrase boundaries and protect combining marks with adequate line-height and padding.

## English review

- Prefer concrete nouns and direct verbs.
- Say what changed, why it matters, what is known, and what happens next.
- Remove generic AI language, inflated adjectives, and abstract stacks.
- Keep sentences short enough to scan without becoming choppy.
- Read aloud for rhythm and remove phrases nobody on the team would naturally say.

## Tone

Be clear, grounded, energetic, and inviting. Energy comes from useful verbs, visible progress, and agency—not hype, exclamation marks, fear, or forced friendliness.

## Quick test

Ask five questions:

1. Can someone tell what this is about immediately?
2. Can they see why the current way is costly or frustrating?
3. Can they see the improvement rather than merely read a claim?
4. Do they know what to try next?
5. Are source, status, boundary, and limitation still intact?
